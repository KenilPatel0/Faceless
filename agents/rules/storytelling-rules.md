# Storytelling & Scene Direction Rules

## Rule 1: Story-First Staging
1. The Story Director decides what the audience should see and feel at each beat before choosing animation techniques.
2. Every scene must reference at least one story beat, have a positive duration, and serve an explicit purpose (hook, context, problem, mechanism, evidence, comparison, escalation, reveal, example, resolution, recap, or CTA).
3. Do not translate every spoken noun into a literal stock photo. Use structured diagrams, authentic screen captures, or deliberate visual metaphors.

## Rule 2: Visual Continuity & Screen Utilization
1. Scenes must use the full widescreen canvas with balanced staging (left stage, center stage, right stage) and clear depth planes.
2. Adjacent shots in one scene must share consistent lighting, palette, and scale unless an intentional world transition occurs.
3. Content layers must NEVER exceed $y = 880\text{px}$ in $1080\text{p}$, guaranteeing an $80\text{px}+$ clearance zone above subtitles at $y = 1010\text{px}$.

## Rule 3: Pacing & Information Absorption
1. Shorts: shots $1.5–4.0\text{s}$, scenes $3.0–9.0\text{s}$.
2. Long-form: shots $3.0–8.0\text{s}$, scenes $6.0–25.0\text{s}$.
3. Important proof screens, terminal commands, and code snippets must be held long enough to be read and understood. Never cut away prematurely.
