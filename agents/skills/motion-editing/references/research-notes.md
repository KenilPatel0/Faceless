# Research notes - motion + editing skill (12 Nov 2026)
Confidence: A = YouTube's own pages; B = documentation of rendering/animation tools; C = blogs/forums/craft practice (directional).

- A. YouTube "Recommended upload encoding settings" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171): MP4; H.264; 4:2:0; keep the frame rate the video was recorded in; SDR 1080p reference bitrate 8 Mbps at 24-30 fps and 12 Mbps at 48-60 fps; no bitrate limit required; moov atom at the front (faststart).
- B/C. Motion blur = temporal sub-frame accumulation; 180-degree shutter (exposure = half a frame) is the film baseline; wider angle can reduce readability of text; per-element control keeps text sharp; sub-frame count must be high enough to avoid visible echoes (Pro Video Coalition "motion blur"; Revideo motion-blur docs; Unity accumulation docs, which also describe jittered sub-frames for anti-aliasing = supersampling). https://www.provideocoalition.com/motion_blur/ , https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@15.0/manual/Accumulation.html
- C. Adobe community answer: fast-moving text judders at 24 fps; 30 fps or a wider shutter angle helps. https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/unwanted-shutter-on-text/m-p/10588067 (forum advice; used as direction only).
- C. Easing/duration conventions (100/300/500 ms; ease-out entrances, ease-in exits, exits ~75%; capped stagger): impeccable motion-design reference and LottieFiles motion-design skill (see humanized skill notes).
- Craft practice (not researched, from standard editing/animation practice): J/L cuts, show-before-say timing, emphasis stack limits, parallax speeds, grain to prevent gradient banding, speed ramps for screen recordings. Treat as defaults to test, not facts.
- Own measurement: the demo (`demo_smooth.py smooth` vs `naive`) shows the difference; with only 6 blur samples a fast card showed visible echo ghosts, which is why `auto_samples` exists.
